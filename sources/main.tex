\begin{abstract}
For every fixed rational number $r$, we prove
$2\leq\mu(\zeta(3)-r\zeta(2))\leq10000$. More precisely, every reduced
rational approximant $a/q$ with sufficiently large denominator satisfies
$|\zeta(3)-r\zeta(2)-a/q|>q^{-10000}$; the threshold may depend on $r$.
The proof extends Tang's determinant construction by estimating the
conditioning of an associated positive Gram matrix. A lower bound
$e^{-60n}$ for its smallest eigenvalue and an upper bound $e^{50n}$ for
the normalized matrix slope give stability on a neighborhood of radius
$e^{-120n}$. The arithmetic-content and prime-reduction estimates of the
construction then turn this local stability into a denominator bound.
The final statements and their dependencies are verified in Lean.
The exponent is explicit but not optimized, and no numerical denominator
threshold is computed.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} irrationality exponent; zeta values;
Diophantine approximation; Gram matrices; determinant estimates;
formal verification.

\section{Introduction}

For an irrational real number $x$, its irrationality exponent is
\[
 \mu(x)=\sup\{\nu>0:0<|x-a/q|<q^{-\nu}
       \text{ for infinitely many reduced }a/q\in\Q,\ q\geq2\}.
\]
The supremum is taken in the extended reals. Dirichlet's theorem gives
$\mu(x)\geq2$. We use the conventional values
$\zeta(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-s}$ for $s=2,3$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every $r\in\Q$, there is an integer $q_0(r)\geq2$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \left|\zeta(3)-r\zeta(2)-\frac aq\right|>q^{-10000}
 \qquad(q\geq q_0(r),\ a\in\Z,\ \gcd(a,q)=1).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
                  2\leq\mu(\zeta(3)-r\zeta(2))\leq10000.
\]
\end{theorem}

The parameter $r$ is fixed before the denominator tends to infinity.
The exponent does not depend on $r$, but no uniform bound for $q_0(r)$
as the height of $r$ grows is asserted. In particular, the theorem is
not a simultaneous linear-independence measure with arbitrarily varying
coefficients of $1,\zeta(2),\zeta(3)$.

The irrationality of $\zeta(3)$ is due to Ap\'ery~\cite{Apery}.
For $r=0$, substantially stronger finite irrationality-exponent bounds
are known; see Rhin and Viola~\cite{RV}. The present extension concerns
the full family with fixed rational $r$.
Dauguet and Zudilin~\cite{DZ} constructed simultaneous approximations to
$\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ and lower bounds for linear forms with specific
arithmetic constraints. Their approximation problem is distinct from
the unrestricted rational approximants in \eqref{eq:main}.

Our starting point is Tang's proof~\cite{Tang} that
$\zeta(3)-r\zeta(2)$ is irrational for every rational $r$, using
Fauzan's determinant method~\cite{Fauzan}. We retain Tang's rational
matrix pencil, primitive integer polynomials, arithmetic-content
estimate, and nonvanishing after reduction modulo a prime. The analytic
argument also uses his pointwise and logarithmic-energy estimates.
These inputs are stated explicitly in Sections~\ref{sec:construction}
and~\ref{sec:energy}.

The additional estimates concern a positive Gram matrix associated with
the signed moment matrix. We retain the positive multiple integral in
the energy calculation and prove an exponential lower bound for its
smallest eigenvalue. A separate residue estimate bounds the normalized
slope. A bilinear determinant comparison then controls the entire
pencil near the target number, without estimates for complementary
minors. Finally, a prime in a logarithmic interval supplies nonvanishing
at each rational approximant and yields \eqref{eq:main}.

Section~\ref{sec:conditioning} proves the conditioning estimate,
Section~\ref{sec:stability} establishes local decay, and
Section~\ref{sec:exponent} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Section~\ref{sec:formal} records the formal statements and reproducible
checks. The Lean source package includes the required upstream sources
at fixed revisions.

\section{The rational matrix pencil}\label{sec:construction}

Fix $r\in\Q$. For $n\geq1$, put
\[
 \theta=\zeta(3)-r\zeta(2),\qquad h=3n,\qquad
 D_m(t)=\prod_{j=1}^m(t+j),\qquad R_n(t)=\frac{D_n(t)^4}{D_{5n}(t)},
\]
and define
\[
 S_n=\frac{(5n)!}{(n!)^4},\qquad F_n=\prod_{j=0}^{h-1}(j!)^2,
 \qquad t(y)=\tfrac12+\ii y,
\]
\[
 w_r(y)=\frac{\pi}{2}\sech^2(\pi y)
       \bigl(2r-2\pi\ii\tanh(\pi y)\bigr),\qquad
 \phi_n(y)=t(y)R_n(t(y))w_r(y).
\]
All matrix indices below run from $0$ to $h-1$.

We give the rational entries explicitly to fix their normalization.
For $k\geq0$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
 t^kR_n(t)=U_{n,k}(t)+\sum_{a=1}^{5n}\frac{c_{n,k,a}}{t+a},
 \qquad
 c_{n,k,a}=\frac{(-a)^kD_n(-a)^4}
                    {\prod_{\substack{1\leq b\leq5n\\b\ne a}}(b-a)},
\end{equation}
where $U_{n,k}\in\Q[t]$ is the polynomial part. Canceled poles have
residue zero. Let $H_a^{(s)}=\sum_{b=1}^a b^{-s}$ and let $B_e^+$ be
the Bernoulli numbers defined by
\[
 \frac{ze^z}{e^z-1}=\sum_{e=0}^{\infty}B_e^+\frac{z^e}{e!};
 \qquad B_1^+=\tfrac12.
\]
Define a rational linear functional and rational coefficients by
\[
 \mathcal L_r(t^e)=(e+1)B_e^++2rB_{e+1}^+,
 \qquad b_{r,a}=2r-2aH_a^{(3)}+2raH_a^{(2)}.
\]
Tang's matrices are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix}
 \begin{split}
 (A_{r,n})_{jk}&=\mathcal L_r(U_{n,j+k})+
                          \sum_{a=1}^{5n}c_{n,j+k,a}b_{r,a},\\
 (B_n)_{jk}&=\sum_{a=1}^{5n}2a\,c_{n,j+k,a}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The polynomial and resolvent moment identities of~\cite{Tang} are
\[
 \int_\R t(y)^{e+1}w_r(y)\dd y=(e+1)B_e^++2rB_{e+1}^+,
 \qquad
 \int_\R\frac{t(y)}{t(y)+a}w_r(y)\dd y=b_{r,a}+2a\theta.
\]
Applying them to \eqref{eq:partial} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entries}
 (A_{r,n}+\theta B_n)_{jk}
       =\int_\R t(y)^{j+k}\phi_n(y)\dd y.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 Q_n(X)=\det(A_{r,n}+XB_n),\qquad
 \widetilde Q_n(X)=\frac{S_n^h}{F_n}Q_n(X).
\]
When $Q_n\ne0$, let $P_n\in\Z[X]$ be its primitive integer multiple
with positive proportionality factor. Thus the greatest common divisor
of the coefficients of $P_n$ is one. Write
$P_n=d_n\widetilde Q_n$ with $d_n\in\Q_{>0}$.
When $Q_n=0$, set $P_n=0$. The determinant expansion gives
$\deg P_n\leq3n$ whenever $P_n\ne0$.

\begin{proposition}[Arithmetic content and prime reduction, Tang]
\label{prop:inputs}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, all sufficiently large $n$ with $Q_n\ne0$
satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arith}
                         \log d_n\leq(283/50+\varepsilon)n^2.
\end{equation}
There is a finite exceptional prime set $E$ such that every prime
$p\geq7$ outside $E$ with $p\nmid\operatorname{den}(r)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
             P_{p-1}(X)\pmod p\quad\text{is a nonzero constant}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

These are the arithmetic-content and prime-edge theorems of~\cite{Tang},
for the definitions above. In particular, \eqref{eq:edge} implies
$P_{p-1}\ne0$, hence $Q_{p-1}\ne0$.
Our $d_n$ is the source's \texttt{dtilde}: it is the primitive multiplier
of the normalized polynomial. It is not the source's multiplier
\texttt{d} of $Q_n$. The distinction accounts for the factor $S_n^h/F_n$
in the analytic estimates.

\section{The positive Gram determinant and its energy bound}
\label{sec:energy}

Set
\[
 f_j(y)=\frac{t(y)^j}{j!},\qquad \rho_n(y)=S_n|\phi_n(y)|,
 \qquad
 M_n=\left(\int_\R\overline{f_j(y)}f_k(y)\rho_n(y)\dd y\right)_{j,k<h}.
\]
Every polynomial moment is integrable: the rational factor has no poles
on $t=1/2+\ii y$, grows at most polynomially, and is multiplied by an
exponentially decreasing hyperbolic kernel. The density is positive
except possibly at $y=0$. For $v\in\C^h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic}
 v^*M_nv=\int_\R\left|\sum_{j<h}v_jf_j(y)\right|^2\rho_n(y)\dd y.
\end{equation}
If $v\ne0$, the polynomial inside the absolute value cannot vanish
on an interval. Hence $M_n$ is Hermitian positive definite. We write
$v^*$ for conjugate transpose and $\|v\|_2^2=\sum_j|v_j|^2$.

Andr\'eief's identity, followed by the Vandermonde determinant formula,
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heine}
 \det M_n=\frac{S_n^h}{F_nh!}
 \int_{\R^h}\prod_{\ell=1}^h|\phi_n(y_\ell)|
                 \prod_{\ell<m}(y_\ell-y_m)^2\dd y.
\end{equation}
Indeed the two determinants of the functions $f_j$ give a squared
absolute Vandermonde divided by $\prod_j(j!)^2=F_n$; the factors of
$\ii$ disappear in its absolute square. Absolute integrability permits
the finite determinant expansion and Fubini's theorem.

Here is the precise positive quantity to which the energy estimates
apply. For $u>0$ set
\[
 W(u)=\frac23\left[\pi u
 -2\left(u\arctan\frac1u+\frac12\log(1+u^2)\right)
 +\frac12\left(u\arctan\frac5u+\frac52\log(1+u^2/25)\right)\right],
\]
with $W(0)=0$. Put
\[
 G_n(x)=\prod_{\ell=1}^h
           (1+|x_\ell|)^7 e^{-3nW(|x_\ell|)}
                       \prod_{\ell<m}(x_\ell-x_m)^2.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise and configuration estimates, Tang]
\label{lem:configuration}
There are constants $c_r,C$, independent of $n$, such that, for $n\geq1$,
\begin{align}
 \rho_n(nx)&\leq e^{c_r+7\log(n+1)}
                    (1+|x|)^7e^{-3nW(|x|)},\label{eq:pointwise}\\
 \int_{\R^{3n}}G_n(x)\dd x
   &\leq\exp\!\left(
       [-6-9(3/2-\log3)]n^2+Cn(\log(n+1)+1)\right).
       \label{eq:configuration}
\end{align}
The integrand in \eqref{eq:configuration} is integrable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use the pointwise and configuration estimates in Tang's energy
construction~\cite{Tang}. For clarity, the latter has the following
form before its constants are combined. Its comparison probability
density on a compact interval has logarithmic energy $I$ and equilibrium
constant $\ell$, with
\[
                  9(3/2-\log3)+9(\ell-I)\leq-6.
\]
The configuration bound supplies constants $B,C_2$ and the majorant
\[
 G_n(x)\leq
 e^{9n^2(\ell-I)+C_2n(\log(n+1)+1)}
 \prod_{j=1}^{3n}(1+|x_j|)^7e^{-3n\max(0,|x_j|-B)}.
\]
Each one-variable factor has integral at most the fixed finite number
\[
 K=\max\left(1,\int_\R(1+|x|)^7e^{-\max(0,|x|-B)}\dd x\right).
\]
Integrating, using the energy inequality, and absorbing
$3n\log K$ into the error proves \eqref{eq:configuration}.
The source results are \texttt{pointwise\_bound},
\texttt{config\_bound}, \texttt{IA\_eq}, and the verified
\texttt{FstarInput} certificate. These estimates concern a nonnegative
integrand, not merely the absolute value of a signed determinant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Positive energy bound]\label{prop:energy}
For fixed $r$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, all sufficiently large $n$ satisfy
\[
                         \det M_n\leq e^{(-6+\varepsilon)n^2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:heine}, substitute $y_j=nx_j$.
The Jacobian contributes $n^h$, and the squared Vandermonde contributes
$n^{h(h-1)}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:pointwise} therefore gives
\[
 \det M_n\leq
 \frac{n^{h^2}e^{h(c_r+7\log(n+1))}}{F_nh!}
                        \int_{\R^h}G_n(x)\dd x.
\]
The factorial sum, or Stirling's estimate summed over $j<h$, gives
\[
 \log F_n=h^2\log h-\tfrac32h^2+O(h\log(h+1)).
\]
With $h=3n$ and $1/h!\leq1$, the logarithm of the prefactor is at most
$9(3/2-\log3)n^2+O_r(n\log(n+1))$.
Combining this with \eqref{eq:configuration} proves
\[
                 \det M_n\leq e^{-6n^2+O_r(n\log(n+1))}.
\]
The error divided by $n^2$ tends to zero, giving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Exponential conditioning of the Gram matrix}
\label{sec:conditioning}

\begin{lemma}[Weight on a distant interval]\label{lem:weight}
For $1\leq x\leq2$ and every $n\geq1$,
\[
                             n\rho_n(nx)\geq e^{-22n},
\]
uniformly in $r\in\Q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $n\leq y\leq2n$, each numerator factor of $R_n(t(y))$ has modulus
at least $n$, and each denominator factor has modulus at most $8n$.
Using $(5n)!\geq(5n/e)^{5n}$ and $n!\leq n^n$ gives
\[
 S_n|R_n(t(y))|\geq\left(\frac5{8e}\right)^{5n}\geq e^{-8n};
\]
the last step follows from $\log(8/5)\leq3/5$.
Also $|t(y)|\geq n$ and $\tanh(\pi y)\geq1/2$.
The imaginary part of $2r-2\pi\ii\tanh(\pi y)$ alone yields
\[
 |w_r(y)|\geq\frac{\pi^2}{2}e^{-2\pi y}\geq e^{-4\pi n},
\]
since $\cosh u\leq e^u$ for $u\geq0$ and $\pi^2/2>1$.
Now $n^2\geq1$ and $8+4\pi<22$ prove the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient recovery]\label{lem:recovery}
Let $v\in\C^h$, where $h=3n$ and $n\geq1$, and put
\[
 p(s)=\sum_{j<h}\frac{v_j}{j!}
                  \left(\frac12+\ii n\frac{3+s}{2}\right)^j.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery}
 \sum_{j<h}|v_j|^2\leq64h^5\,111132^h
                             \int_{-1}^1|p(s)|^2\dd s.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $M=\max_{[-1,1]}|p|$. We first recall the coefficient consequence
of the endpoint Chebyshev derivative inequality. If a real polynomial
$g$ has degree at most $h$ and $|g|\leq M$ on $[-1,1]$, then
\[
 |g^{(j)}(1)|\leq M T_h^{(j)}(1),
 \qquad |[s^i]g|\leq M T_h(3)\leq7^hM.
\]
For the middle inequality, expand $g$ at $1$, bound the coefficients of
$(s-1)^j$ by $2^j$, and sum the nonnegative Taylor coefficients of
$T_h(1+z)$ at $z=2$. The recurrence
$T_{h+2}(3)=6T_{h+1}(3)-T_h(3)$ gives the last bound by induction.
The derivative inequality is the standard extremal property of
Chebyshev polynomials, also used in the formal proof.
Apply this argument to the real and imaginary coefficient polynomials
of $p$. Each has absolute value at most $M$ on the interval, so
\[
                            |[s^j]p|\leq2\,7^hM.
\]
For $s,t\in[-1,1]$, the identity for $s^j-t^j$ gives
$|s^j-t^j|\leq j|s-t|$. Consequently
\[
                      |p(s)-p(t)|\leq2h^2\,7^hM|s-t|.
\]
At a point where $|p|=M$, at least one direction has distance at least
$1$ to the endpoint of $[-1,1]$. Along that direction take an interval
of length $1/(4h^2 7^h)$. On this interval $|p|\geq M/2$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peak}
                    M^2\leq16h^2\,7^h\int_{-1}^1|p(s)|^2\dd s.
\end{equation}
If $M=0$, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise define
\[
 q(z)=\sum_{j<h}\frac{v_jn^j}{j!}z^j
                      =p(-3+\ii/n-2\ii z).
\]
The affine constant has modulus at most $4$ and the slope has modulus
$2$. Every coefficient of its $j$th power has modulus at most $6^j$.
Summing over the at most $h$ coefficients of $p$ yields
$|[z^j]q|\leq2h42^hM$.
Since $j!/n^j\leq3^h$ for $j<h=3n$, we obtain
$|v_j|\leq2h126^hM$. Squaring and summing, then applying
\eqref{eq:peak}, proves \eqref{eq:recovery}, because
$126^2\cdot7=111132$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gram lower bound]\label{prop:conditioning}
There is $N$ independent of $r$ such that, for all $n\geq N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditioning}
                     v^*M_nv\geq e^{-60n}\|v\|_2^2
                     \qquad(v\in\C^{3n}).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\lambda_{\min}(M_n)\geq e^{-60n}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Restrict \eqref{eq:quadratic} to $y\in[n,2n]$ and substitute
$y=n(3+s)/2$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:weight} and~\ref{lem:recovery} give
\[
 v^*M_nv\geq\frac{e^{-22n}}2\int_{-1}^1|p(s)|^2\dd s
          \geq\frac{e^{-22n}}{128h^5\,111132^h}\|v\|_2^2.
\]
Since $111132<e^{12}$ and $\log n=o(n)$,
$128(3n)^5\,111132^{3n}\leq e^{38n}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
Neither this threshold nor the preceding constants depend on $r$.
\end{proof}

\section{Slope growth and local stability}\label{sec:stability}

Normalize the whole pencil, for real $X$, by
\[
 H_n(X)_{jk}=\frac{S_n}{j!k!}(A_{r,n}+XB_n)_{jk},\qquad
 T_n=H_n'(X).
\]
The row and column factorials give the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pencil}
                         \det H_n(X)=\widetilde Q_n(X).
\end{equation}
It holds for every $X$, not only for $X=\theta$.

\begin{lemma}[Normalized slope]\label{lem:slope}
For $n\geq1$ and $0\leq j,k<3n$,
\[
                              |(T_n)_{jk}|\leq e^{50n}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Formula~\eqref{eq:partial} gives, for $1\leq a\leq5n$,
\[
 S_nc_{n,0,a}=(-1)^{a-1}5n
                  \binom{5n-1}{a-1}\binom{a-1}{n}^{\!4}.
\]
This also holds for $a\leq n$, when both sides vanish. The elementary
bound $\binom uv\leq2^u$ yields $|S_nc_{n,0,a}|\leq5n\,2^{25n}$.
Since $c_{n,j+k,a}=(-a)^{j+k}c_{n,0,a}$, \eqref{eq:matrix} implies
\[
 |(T_n)_{jk}|\leq250n^3\,2^{25n}
                          \frac{(5n)^j}{j!}\frac{(5n)^k}{k!}.
\]
Each factorial ratio is at most $e^{5n}$, by the exponential series.
Also $250\leq e^{8n}$, $n^3\leq e^{3n}$, and $2^{25n}\leq e^{25n}$.
Thus the displayed bound is at most $e^{46n}\leq e^{50n}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bilinear Gram comparison]\label{lem:comparison}
Let $M$ be a positive definite Hermitian $h\times h$ matrix and $B$ a
complex $h\times h$ matrix. If $C\geq0$ and
\[
 |u^{\mathsf T}Bv|\leq C\sqrt{u^*Mu}\sqrt{v^*Mv}
                       \qquad(u,v\in\C^h),
\]
then $|\det B|\leq\det M\,C^h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an invertible $W$ with $W^*MW=I$, and set $L=W^{\mathsf T}BW$.
Applying the hypothesis to $Wu,Wv$ gives
$|u^{\mathsf T}Lv|\leq C\|u\|_2\|v\|_2$.
Take $u=\overline{Lv}$ to obtain
$\|Lv\|_2^2\leq C\|Lv\|_2\|v\|_2$, so $\|L\|_{2\to2}\leq C$.
Hadamard's inequality gives $|\det L|\leq C^h$.
Taking determinants in $W^*MW=I$ yields
$|\det W|^2\det M=1$, while
$|\det L|=|\det W|^2|\det B|$. The result follows.
The transpose in $W^{\mathsf T}BW$ is intentional: the form being
estimated is bilinear, not sesquilinear.
\end{proof}

For $F_v(y)=\sum_jv_jf_j(y)$, equation~\eqref{eq:entries} gives
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}H_n(\theta)v
                    =\int_\R F_u(y)F_v(y)S_n\phi_n(y)\dd y.
\]
Since $|S_n\phi_n|=\rho_n$, Cauchy--Schwarz and
\eqref{eq:quadratic} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed}
 |u^{\mathsf T}H_n(\theta)v|
                    \leq\sqrt{u^*M_nu}\sqrt{v^*M_nv}.
\end{equation}
All square integrals are finite by the moment integrability established
in Section~\ref{sec:energy}.

If $E$ is a matrix with $|E_{jk}|\leq K$, finite Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 |u^{\mathsf T}Ev|\leq K\Bigl(\sum_j|u_j|\Bigr)
                          \Bigl(\sum_k|v_k|\Bigr)
               \leq hK\|u\|_2\|v\|_2.
\]
For $n$ satisfying Proposition~\ref{prop:conditioning}, this is at most
$hKe^{60n}\sqrt{u^*M_nu}\sqrt{v^*M_nv}$.
Combine this with \eqref{eq:signed} and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:comparison} to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbation}
       |\det(H_n(\theta)+E)|\leq\det M_n(1+hKe^{60n})^h.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Local decay]\label{prop:local}
For fixed $r$, all sufficiently large $n$ with $Q_n\ne0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 |x-\theta|\leq e^{-120n}
              \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |P_n(x)|\leq e^{-3n^2/10}
              \qquad(x\in\R).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use $\varepsilon=1/100$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:energy} and
\eqref{eq:arith}. Set $E=H_n(x)-H_n(\theta)$ in
\eqref{eq:perturbation}. Lemma~\ref{lem:slope} permits
$K=|x-\theta|e^{50n}\leq e^{-70n}$. Hence
\[
 |\widetilde Q_n(x)|\leq e^{-599n^2/100}(1+3ne^{-10n})^{3n}
                     \leq e^{-599n^2/100+3n}.
\]
The last step uses $3n\leq e^{10n}$ and $2^{3n}\leq e^{3n}$.
Multiplying by $d_n\leq e^{567n^2/100}$ gives
\[
                         |P_n(x)|\leq e^{-8n^2/25+3n}.
\]
For $n\geq150$, the exponent is at most $-3n^2/10$.
\end{proof}

\section{Prime selection and the denominator bound}\label{sec:exponent}

\begin{lemma}[A prime avoiding a denominator]\label{lem:prime}
For every fixed cutoff $P_0$, all sufficiently large positive integers
$q$ admit a prime $p>P_0$ such that
\[
                    20\log q<p\leq60\log q,\qquad p\nmid q.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\vartheta(u)=\sum_{p\leq u}\log p$.
Elementary Chebyshev estimates give
\[
 \vartheta(u)\leq2(\log2)u,\qquad
 \vartheta(u)\geq(\log2)u-O(\sqrt u\log(u+2)).
\]
Consequently
$\vartheta(3u)-\vartheta(u)\geq(\log2)u-o(u)>u/4$
for sufficiently large $u$.
Take $u=20\log q$. If every prime in $(u,3u]$ divided $q$, their
product would divide $q$, so the sum of their logarithms would be at
most $\log q$. Its lower bound $5\log q$ contradicts this.
Finally $u\to\infty$ places the window above $P_0$.
Only elementary prime estimates are needed for this selection step.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Fix $r$. Choose $P_0$ above $7$, every exceptional prime in
\eqref{eq:edge}, $\operatorname{den}(r)$, and all fixed thresholds
needed for Proposition~\ref{prop:local}, increased by one.
For sufficiently large $q$, take $p$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:prime} and
put $n=p-1$. Increasing the threshold for $q$ if necessary gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
                         19\log q<n\leq60\log q.
\end{equation}
Since $p\nmid\operatorname{den}(r)$, \eqref{eq:edge} applies and gives
$Q_n\ne0$. Suppose that a reduced rational $a/q$ satisfies
$|\theta-a/q|\leq q^{-10000}$. By \eqref{eq:window},
\[
 q^{-10000}=e^{-10000\log q}\leq e^{-120n},
\]
because $120\cdot60=7200<10000$. Proposition~\ref{prop:local} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:too-small}
                    |P_n(a/q)|\leq e^{-3n^2/10}<q^{-3n};
\end{equation}
the strict inequality uses $n>10\log q$.

On the other hand, reduction modulo $p$ evaluates $P_n$ to a nonzero
constant. The denominator $q$ is invertible modulo $p$, so $P_n(a/q)$
cannot vanish. Since $P_n\in\Z[X]$ has degree at most $3n$,
\[
                          q^{3n}P_n(a/q)\in\Z\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Thus $|P_n(a/q)|\geq q^{-3n}$, contradicting \eqref{eq:too-small}.
This proves \eqref{eq:main} for every sufficiently large reduced
denominator, not merely for a selected sequence.

Tang's theorem supplies the irrationality of $\theta$, so Dirichlet's
theorem gives $\mu(\theta)\geq2$. For $\nu\geq10000$,
\eqref{eq:main} excludes all sufficiently large reduced denominators
from $0<|\theta-a/q|<q^{-\nu}$. For the finitely many remaining
denominators only finitely many numerators can satisfy this inequality.
The defining set of approximation exponents is therefore bounded above
by $10000$, proving the upper bound.
\end{proof}

The numerical choices have substantial slack: local stability requires
only the comparison with $7200$ above, and the integer-polynomial
contradiction needs $n>10\log q$, whereas the prime window gives
$n>19\log q$. We keep $10000$ as the stated and formally checked
exponent. Computing $q_0(r)$ or optimizing these constants is a separate
problem.

\section{Formal verification and reproducibility}\label{sec:formal}

The two final declarations, in the namespace \texttt{Zeta32Extension}, are
\begin{center}
 \texttt{denominator\_bound},\qquad
 \texttt{irrationalityExponent\_bounds}.
\end{center}
The first asserts, for every $r:\Q$, that eventually in natural $b$,
every rational $z$ with reduced denominator $b$ satisfies
\[
 b^{-10000}<|\operatorname{Cr}(r)-z|,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cr}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(k+1)^3}
                   -r\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(k+1)^2}.
\]
Thus the formal target is the literal series expression in the theorem.
There is no analytic, arithmetic, matrix-rank, or nonvanishing hypothesis
left in either endpoint. The exponent is defined as a real supremum;
before using it the code proves that its defining set contains $2$ and
is bounded above. It therefore agrees here with the extended-real
definition in Section~1.
